Emergency drain cleaning in Johnstown, CO

When a drain backs up in Johnstown, CO, the first move is simple. Stop running water, leave the toilet unflushed, and call (970) 708-6013. Emergency drain cleaning is available 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays, and the dispatcher can tell you what to do while a plumber is on the way.

Drain cleaning means removing the blockage and then confirming the pipe flows freely again. That covers one clogged kitchen sink, a slow bathtub, a floor drain in a garage or basement, and a main line that pushes water back up through every fixture in the house.

A good visit also answers a second question: why did it clog? Grease, hair, wipes and roots each leave different clues, and the fix that lasts depends on which one it was.

When a clogged drain is an emergency

Slow drains can wait for a morning appointment. Water coming back up cannot, so use this order while you wait for the plumber.

  • Stop using every fixture on that line. Each flush or load of laundry adds water that has nowhere to go.
  • If wastewater is rising in a tub, shower or floor drain, treat the main line as blocked. Keep children and pets out of the wet area.
  • Lift rugs, boxes and electronics off the floor before the water reaches them, and stay clear of outlets near standing water.
  • Skip the bottle of chemical drain cleaner. Poured into a standing backup it can splash, and it makes the repair harder on whoever handles the line next.
  • Find the cleanout, usually a capped pipe in the basement, crawl space or outside near the foundation. Leave the cap on and tell the plumber where it is.

Night and weekend calls reach the same 24/7 line. The plumber clears the blockage, runs water to confirm the line drains, and explains what caused it.

Signs the clog is in the main line, not one fixture

Where the symptoms show up tells you how deep the clog sits. Check these before you call so the dispatcher can send the right equipment.

  • Two or more fixtures drain slowly at the same time.
  • A tub or toilet gurgles when the washing machine or a sink drains.
  • Water shows up in a basement floor drain after laundry.
  • The toilet bowl rises or burps when the dishwasher runs.
  • A sewage smell hangs around floor drains or the cleanout.

One slow sink is usually a trap or a short branch line. Several symptoms together point to the main, and that is where a cable machine and a camera come in.

Kitchen sink clogs: the call we get most

Kitchen lines carry grease, starch and food scraps, so they clog more often than any other drain in the house. Fat cools inside the pipe and coats the walls a little more with every dishwasher cycle until the opening is the size of a pencil.

A plumber starts at the trap, then works down the branch line with a cable until water runs clear. If the sink shares a line with a disposal, the disposal gets checked too, since a jammed unit can look like a clog. Garbage disposal repair covers that side of the job.

When the same sink clogs every few weeks, a cable only reopens the pipe. Scouring the grease off the walls with hydro jetting is what stops the repeat calls.

Tub, shower and floor drain clogs

Bathtub and shower drains clog with hair and soap scum that wrap around the stopper and the first few inches of pipe. Most of these clear with a hand tool, and a plumber can pull the stopper assembly to find out whether the clog is local or whether it travels farther down.

Floor drains in garages and basements are different. They sit at the lowest point of the house and often share a line with the main, so water in a floor drain during a heavy laundry day can be the first sign of a blocked main.

Water that appears when no fixture has been used is a different problem. A leaking drain line can wet a ceiling or a wall, and leak detection is the right next step instead of more cabling.

What changes the price of drain cleaning

Every blockage is a little different, so the price depends on the job in front of the plumber.

  • Where the clog sits A trap under one sink is quick work. A blockage 80 feet down the main takes more time and more cable.
  • Access to the line A working cleanout lets the plumber start at the main. Without one, a toilet may need to be pulled to reach the pipe.
  • The method used A hand tool, a cable machine, a jetter and a camera each take different time and equipment.
  • The cause Grease and hair clear differently from roots or a collapsed section of pipe.
  • When you call An emergency at 2 a.m. is dispatched differently from a booked weekday visit.

Keeping the line clear after the visit
