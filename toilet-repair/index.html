When a toilet problem is an emergency

Most toilet trouble is annoying. Some of it is urgent, and these steps help while the plumber is on the way.

  • Reach behind the toilet and turn the small shutoff valve clockwise until the water stops flowing.
  • If the bowl is rising, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or lift the float, to stop more water entering.
  • Do not flush again, even to test. Each flush can push more water out.
  • Put towels around the base and move rugs and anything electrical away from the wet area.
  • If water is rising in other fixtures, stop using all of them and treat the problem as a main line issue.

Toilet repair and installation in Johnstown, CO

A toilet is simple on the outside and fussy on the inside. Most problems come down to a handful of parts, such as the fill valve, the flapper, the wax ring at the floor, the supply line and the flange that holds the whole thing down.

The plumber starts by finding where the problem begins, then repairs it or says honestly that a new toilet makes more sense. Toilet installation is part of the same service, for a swap of an old unit or a new one set in a remodeled bathroom.

The toilet that will not stop running

A running toilet wastes water around the clock and often shows up first as a higher water bill. The cause is almost always a flapper that no longer seals or a fill valve that fails to shut off at the right level.

Both parts are inexpensive, and a plumber can usually swap them in a short visit. A trickle that goes on all night is worth fixing soon, since you pay for every gallon even though nobody sees it. If the water bill is high and no toilet seems to run, leak detection can check the rest of the house.

Leaks at the base and the wax ring

A toilet sits on a wax or foam ring that seals it to the drain. When that ring fails, water seeps out at the base during a flush and can soak into the subfloor before anyone notices.

A damp floor, a soft spot near the toilet, or a stain on the ceiling below are signs to act soon. Resetting the toilet with a new seal is a routine repair, but the longer water leaks, the more flooring may need replacing too.

Rocking is a related warning. A toilet that moves under you has a loose or damaged flange, and the seal will keep failing until the flange is fixed.

Replacing or installing a toilet

  1. Choose the right toilet.

    Height, bowl shape, rough-in distance and flush type all affect fit and comfort.

  2. Remove the old one.

    The water is shut off, the tank is drained, and the old toilet and seal are pulled.

  3. Check the flange and floor.

    A damaged flange or soft floor is repaired before the new toilet goes down.

  4. Set and test.

    The new toilet is leveled, secured, connected to the supply and tested with several flushes.

A bathroom remodel with new fixtures and moved plumbing may be better handled as plumbing installation work.

What to do before the plumber arrives when a toilet overflows

Most overflows can be slowed in under a minute if you know where to reach. Do these in order.

  • Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or turn the small valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the refill.
  • Do not flush again, and stop using other fixtures on the same line if the bowl is rising.
  • Put towels around the base, lift rugs, and keep children and pets out of the water.
  • Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a full bowl, since it can splash back.
  • Call the 24/7 line if water keeps coming and the shutoff will not close.

Clogs, backups and when it is more than the toilet

A single clogged toilet usually clears with a plunger or a closet auger. If the toilet backs up again and again, or another drain gurgles when it flushes, the blockage is farther along the line.

At that point the problem belongs to drain cleaning or, if the main is blocked, sewer line cleaning. Telling the dispatcher what else in the house is slow helps the plumber bring the right tools the first time.

Small habits that protect your toilets

  • Flush toilet paper only, and put wipes, cotton and hygiene products in the trash.
  • Check the tank for slow leaks by adding a few drops of food coloring and waiting.
  • Replace a stiff, worn supply line before it fails behind the toilet.
  • Know where each shutoff valve is, and turn it now and then so it does not seize.
  • Fix a wobbly toilet right away instead of waiting for the floor to rot.
