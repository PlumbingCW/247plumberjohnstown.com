What Do I Need to Install a Garbage Disposal in Johnstown, CO?

A disposal swap looks simple until the sink, the power and the drain do not match the box. Here is the shopping list and the checks to run first.

Call (970) 708-6013

By the Johnstown 24/7 Plumbing team · Published 2026-10-13 · Updated 2026-10-13

Start with what is under your sink

Most people shop for the disposal first and look under the sink second. That order causes the returns. Open the cabinet, clear it out and look at three things before you buy anything: the drain opening in the sink, the space below it and where the power comes from.

A standard kitchen sink has a round drain opening that a disposal mounting ring fits into. Double-bowl sinks, older stainless sinks and some composite sinks can have a mounting ring that does not match the new unit. Take a photo of the underside and keep it on your phone when you shop.

The shopping list

Buying a disposal does not always mean buying everything you need. These are the items that most often turn a one-hour job into a second trip to the store.

  • The disposal itself, sized to your household and the horsepower your wiring can support.
  • A power cord kit if the unit does not come with a cord, or the right connector if it will be hardwired.
  • A new discharge tube and the slip nuts and washers that join it to the trap.
  • Plumber's putty or the gasket your model calls for at the sink flange.
  • A dishwasher drain hose clamp if a dishwasher ties into the disposal.
  • A bucket, a flashlight, pliers, a screwdriver set and a hacksaw for trimming pipe.

Check the manual for any extra part it names. Brands differ, and the list in the box is the one to trust over a general guide.

Power is the part that stops DIY jobs

A disposal needs electricity under the sink. Some kitchens already have a switched outlet there, some have a wired switch on the wall with a cable ready for the unit, and some have nothing at all.

If the old unit was plugged in, the new one can usually be plugged in the same way. If there is no power under the sink, adding it is electrical work, and that is a licensed electrician's job rather than a plumber's. Turn the breaker off before you touch any wiring, and test that the circuit is dead.

A unit that trips the breaker as soon as it starts is not an installation quirk. Stop using it and have it checked.

How the install runs, start to finish

Knowing the order helps you decide whether this is your afternoon or a plumber's visit.

  1. Cut power and clear the cabinet.

    Switch off the breaker, unplug or disconnect the old unit and clear the cabinet floor.

  2. Remove the old disposal.

    Disconnect the discharge tube and dishwasher hose, then twist the unit off its mounting ring.

  3. Set the new flange.

    Press putty or the gasket under the sink flange, seat it and tighten the mounting assembly evenly.

  4. Hang and connect the unit.

    Lift the disposal onto the ring, align the outlet with the trap and reconnect the hose and discharge tube.

  5. Fill and test.

    Run water, run the unit and watch every joint for drips before you close the cabinet.

The lifting and the overhead tightening are the awkward part. If the unit is heavy or the cabinet is cramped, a second person or a plumber saves a lot of bruised knuckles.

When to hand it to a plumber

A simple swap with power already in place is a fair DIY job. Call a plumber when the old unit will not come off, the trap needs re-plumbing, the sink is cracked or the dishwasher connection looks wrong.
