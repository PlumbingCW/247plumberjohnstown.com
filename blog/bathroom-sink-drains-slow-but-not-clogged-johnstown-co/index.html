Bathroom Sink Drains Slow But Not Clogged in Johnstown, CO

Bathroom sinks slow down differently from kitchen sinks. Start with the stopper assembly and work your way down the pipe.

Call (970) 708-6013

By the Johnstown 24/7 Plumbing team · Published 2026-10-13 · Updated 2026-10-13

Start at the stopper, not the pipe

A bathroom sink collects hair, toothpaste, soap and skin oils, and almost all of it settles in the first few inches of the drain. That is also where the pop-up stopper lives. A stopper that does not lift all the way, or one with a hair mat wrapped around it, slows the sink as much as a clog would.

Before you grab a drain product, open the stopper and look. It takes ten minutes and costs nothing.

Clean the pop-up stopper

Most bathroom sink stoppers lift out or unscrew from below. This is the usual order.

  1. Look for the lift rod.

    The thin rod behind the faucet moves the stopper through a pivot rod under the sink.

  2. Release the pivot.

    Under the sink, loosen the nut where the pivot rod enters the tailpiece, then slide the rod out.

  3. Lift out the stopper.

    Pull the stopper straight up. Hair and a gray film are usually wrapped around it.

  4. Wipe and clear.

    Clean the stopper and reach into the drain opening with a hair-grabbing tool or a bent wire.

  5. Reassemble.

    Replace the stopper with the notch facing the pivot rod, tighten the nut by hand and test it.

If the stopper pops up halfway because the linkage is out of adjustment, the sink drains slowly even when it is spotless. Check how far it lifts.

The overflow channel and soap scale

Look at the small opening near the top of the basin. That overflow channel runs down inside the sink and joins the drain below the stopper, so it can hold the same grime you just cleaned out, and you cannot see it.

Soap scum hardens into a gummy scale on the inside of small pipes. A thin brush and warm soapy water clear the overflow, and a gentle rinse flushes the loosened material. Skip the strong products, which can damage chrome and plastic parts.

Cleaned the stopper and it is still slow? Call the 24/7 line. The cause may be farther down than you can reach.

Older bathrooms near Parish Avenue

The original Johnstown townsite was platted in 1902, and homes near Parish Avenue are the likeliest to have older sink drains, small-diameter pipe and bathrooms that have been remodeled around the original lines. In a bathroom like that, a slow sink may share a drain run with other fixtures in a way that is not obvious.

Ask whoever remodeled the room what lies behind the wall. A plumber who knows the layout can pick between a hand tool at the trap and a cable through the branch, and can say whether a camera makes sense.

Bathrooms in newer Johnstown subdivisions tend to have simpler layouts, so a slow sink there is more likely a stopper or trap problem than anything buried.

Pulling the trap, carefully

If the stopper and overflow are clean, the next suspect is the P-trap, the curved pipe under the basin. Put a bucket underneath, loosen the slip nuts by hand, and tip the trap into the bucket.

A trap full of gray sludge is a good sign, because you have found the problem. Rinse it, reconnect it hand-tight plus a quarter turn, and run water to check for drips. If the trap is clean, the restriction lies farther along the line and a plumber's cable reaches it.

A persistent slow bathroom sink is a job for drain cleaning, and a bathroom sink that leaks around the faucet or supply lines falls under faucet repair.

Habits that keep a bathroom basin draining

A few small routines stop most repeat slowdowns, and none of them need tools.

  • Rinse the basin with hot water after shaving or brushing so loose hair and paste do not settle.
  • Lift the stopper and wipe it with a cloth every month or two.
  • Use a mesh drain strainer if the household has long hair.
  • Keep hair products and oils away from the basin, since they harden in the tailpiece.
  • Run hot water for a full minute at the end of the day to soften soap film.

Take a quick photo of the stopper each time you clean it, so you can see how fast the buildup returns. If the sink slows again within a few weeks of cleaning, the buildup is deeper than the stopper, and that is the point to book a cable visit instead of repeating the same cleaning.

Bathroom drains in the neighboring towns

We see the same stopper and scale pattern in homes across the area. Homeowners in Milliken and Mead call about slow bathroom sinks more often than any other drain complaint, since one household uses the basin several times a day.

If one basin is slow in the master bath and not in the guest bath, you can rule out the main line. If every bathroom is slow, mention that on the call.
