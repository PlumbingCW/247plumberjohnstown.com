Hydro Jetting Sewer Line Cost in Johnstown, CO

Jetting scours a sewer line clean with high-pressure water. Here is what drives the cost and when a cable is enough.

What hydro jetting is, and what it is not

Hydro jetting uses a hose with a nozzle that fires high-pressure water in all directions inside the pipe. It cuts through grease, scale and roots, then flushes the debris down the line. It cleans the whole wall of the pipe, which a cable cannot do.

It is not a repair. Jetting cleans a pipe that is sound. It will not fix a crack, a sag or a collapsed section, and it should not be used on pipe that cannot take the pressure.

Because the work varies so much from one line to the next, any dollar range printed without a visit would be a guess. This post explains what changes the price.

What changes the cost of a jetting visit

These are the factors a plumber looks at before quoting.

  • Length of the line A short run from the cleanout to the street takes less time than a long run or a line with several branches.
  • How much is in the pipe Light grease is quick work. Heavy scale, packed roots or years of buildup take longer.
  • Access A working cleanout lets the plumber start fast. Without one, a toilet may need to be pulled or another opening made.
  • Camera inspection A camera before and after shows the cause and confirms the result, but adds time and equipment.
  • Pipe condition Cracked or offset sections can limit the pressure used or change the plan.
  • Equipment Small residential lines and large commercial lines call for different jetters.
  • Time of the call An emergency at night is dispatched differently from a booked daytime visit.

When a cable is enough

A first-time clog in a line that has been trouble-free is often a cable job. The plumber breaks through the blockage, runs water to confirm flow and tells you what caused it. That is quicker and often less costly than jetting.

Jetting makes sense when the same line clogs again, when the camera shows heavy buildup on the walls, or when roots have returned after cutting. The goal is to scour the pipe so the clog does not come back in weeks.

A good plumber will tell you which one the line needs. If a visit always ends in the same upsell, ask why and ask to see the camera footage.

Questions to ask before you book

Ask whether the quote includes a camera inspection, and whether you can see the footage. Ask whether the plumber has a cleanout to work from and what happens if there is none.

Ask what the plumber will do if the camera shows a cracked or collapsed pipe. A responsible plumber stops, shows you the damage and talks through sewer line repair options instead of jetting through it.

Responsibility for the sewer line divides at some point between your house and the main. Ask the Town of Johnstown public works where that point sits for your address, because it affects who pays for a problem near the street.

What a visit looks like on site

The plumber starts by finding the cleanout, usually a capped pipe in the basement, crawl space or outside near the foundation. A camera often goes in first to show what is in the line and how far it runs.

Next the jetting hose goes in, and the nozzle works its way down the pipe while water scours the walls. The plumber backs it out slowly, which pulls debris toward the cleanout or down to the main.

A second camera pass confirms the walls are clean and shows whether any damage was hiding behind the buildup. Ask to see both passes, because the footage is what turns a quote into something you can check.

After the jetting

Keep the line clean by not flushing wipes, pouring grease down the sink or washing food scraps into the drain. A hair catcher in each tub helps too.

If you run a business, ask about scheduled cleaning. Preventive jetting costs less than a night call for a backup in the middle of service.
