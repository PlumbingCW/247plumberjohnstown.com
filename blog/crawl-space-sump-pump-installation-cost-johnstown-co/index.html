Crawl Space Sump Pump Installation Cost: A Johnstown, CO Guide

Crawl space pumps look small, but the work around them is not. This guide explains what shapes the price so you can read a quote with confidence.

By the Johnstown 24/7 Plumbing team · Published 2026-10-13 · Updated 2026-10-13

Why a crawl space job is its own thing

A sump pump in a crawl space does the same job as one in a basement. It collects water in a pit and pushes it out of the house. The difference is the work. A crawl space has little headroom, usually a dirt or thin concrete floor, and often a vapor barrier that has to be cut and sealed again.

That changes the price. Time spent crawling, hauling a pit liner and digging in a cramped space adds up fast, and it is the main reason a crawl space quote does not match a basement quote.

Real numbers need a plumber who has crawled in and looked. This post sticks to what drives the price, because any dollar range printed without a visit would be a guess.

What drives the price of a crawl space pump

Read any quote against these factors. If one is missing from the written scope, ask about it.

  • Digging the pit Breaking through concrete or digging in soil, then setting a liner at the right depth, takes more effort in a tight space.
  • Pump type A submersible pump sits in the pit. A pedestal pump sits above it. Horsepower, switch type and build quality all change the cost.
  • Discharge line The pipe that carries water out has to run to a spot where it will not return to the foundation. Longer runs and trenching cost more.
  • Power A pump needs a nearby outlet on a protected circuit. Running a new line or adding a GFCI adds electrical work.
  • Backup A battery or water-powered backup pump adds parts and labor, and it covers you in an outage.
  • Vapor barrier and sealing Cutting and resealing the barrier around the pit keeps moisture from coming back into the space.
  • Access A small hatch or a long crawl to the work area slows everything down.

Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage but not groundwater seepage or a pump that wears out, and sump pump overflow is often a separate endorsement. The Johnstown area and heavy-water seasons

Johnstown sits west of where the Big Thompson and Little Thompson rivers meet, at about 4,994 feet. Both rivers feed this part of Weld and Larimer counties, and the September 2013 floods affected about 3,000 homes in Weld County. Nearby Greeley saw a hailstorm and flooding on May 28, 2024.

Those events are a reminder that water can reach a crawl space even in a place that is dry most weeks. If your home has a crawl space, look inside after the next hard rain or during spring snowmelt. Standing water, damp insulation or a musty smell means you need a pump or a repair to the existing one.

Do not wait for a flood to test a pump. Pour a bucket of water in the pit and watch it start and empty. If it does not run, call before the weather turns.

Call 811 before the discharge line goes in

A discharge line often means digging outside. In Colorado, anyone digging must call 811 at least two full business days before starting. The call is free, and it marks buried gas, electric, water and sewer lines.

Ask your installer whether a locate request has been placed and when it will be ready. If a contractor says they do not need one for a shallow trench, that is a warning sign.

Also ask where the water will go. It should empty well away from the foundation and not onto a neighbor's lot or into the sewer line. Rules on discharge can differ, so ask the Town of Johnstown building department what applies.

Pump choices that change the quote

A submersible pump sits down in the pit, runs quieter and handles heavier flow. A pedestal pump sits above the pit on a stalk, costs less up front and is easier to reach, but it is louder and can wear faster.

The float switch matters as much as the pump. A switch that sticks leaves the pump off when water rises or on when it should not be. A vertical float or an electronic switch is less likely to snag in a small pit.

If the crawl space is in a house with finished rooms above, a backup is worth pricing. A backup pump turns an outage into an inconvenience instead of a flood. See our sump pump service page for what we install and repair.

Pairing a pump with drainage and moisture control

A pump moves water that has already gathered. It does not keep water out. If your crawl space is wet most of the year, a plumber may also suggest a drainage line, a better vapor barrier or work on the gutters and grading around the house.

For a wider problem, basement waterproofing covers interior drainage and the pumps that go with it. Sometimes the cheapest fix is outside, where the water enters.
