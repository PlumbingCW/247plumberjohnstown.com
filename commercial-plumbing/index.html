Emergency commercial plumbing for Johnstown businesses

When water is spreading across a shop floor or a restaurant kitchen drain stops, every hour closed costs money. Call (970) 708-6013 and the 24/7 line will get a plumber moving. Emergency commercial plumbing is available nights, weekends and holidays.

Commercial work differs from a house call in a few ways. The pipes are larger, the fixtures are used much harder, and the building often has more than one tenant or more than one person who decides what gets done. A good first call covers all of that in a couple of minutes.

What to tell the dispatcher on a business call

A few details let the plumber arrive with the right equipment and the right access.

  • The type of business and the street address.
  • What failed, whether it is a drain, a pipe, a fixture or a water heater.
  • Whether water is still running and whether you have shut off the supply.
  • Who can let the plumber into the building and where the main shutoff is.
  • Whether the building has other tenants who could be affected.
  • Whether the owner, manager or tenant approves the work and who pays.

If you do not know the answer to some of these, call anyway. The dispatcher can still send help while you find out.

When a commercial plumbing problem is an emergency

Some failures can wait for a morning appointment. These cannot, so act on them while the plumber is on the way.

  • Water flooding a floor, a stockroom or an electrical room.
  • A floor drain overflowing with wastewater or kitchen waste.
  • A burst pipe or a failed valve that will not close.
  • Restrooms that cannot be used, which may force a business to close.
  • A grease trap or main line that is backing up into the building.

Shut off the nearest valve or the main if you can reach it safely, keep people out of wet areas near outlets, and move inventory off the floor. A restaurant or a shop with a failed restroom may need to close until the repair is done.

Restaurants, kitchens and floor drains

Food service places punish drains. Grease from dishwashing and cooking cools in the pipe and coats the walls, and floor drains in a kitchen carry wash water all day long. When a floor drain starts to back up, the cause is usually grease packed in the branch line or in the trap.

A plumber can cable or jet the line to restore flow, and in many cases suggest a cleaning routine that fits how the kitchen runs. For the underlying methods see drain cleaning and hydro jetting.

Grease traps and interceptors are a separate task. If one is overfull, mention it on the call so the plumber can plan the visit.

Commercial leak detection, an honest word

Finding a hidden leak in a commercial building is harder than in a house. Larger buildings have longer pipe runs, more fixtures and sometimes slab areas that are difficult to reach. Detection can pinpoint many leaks, but not every one can be found in a single visit.

The plumber will explain what can be located with the equipment on site and what might call for a second look. If a leak turns out to be inside a wall or under a slab, the next step is a repair plan rather than a quick fix. Leak detection explains the methods in more detail.

What to do before the plumber arrives at your business

Protecting people and stock comes first. These steps apply whether the problem is a backup, a burst line or a dead water heater.

  • Shut off the main water valve or the valve serving the affected area, and tell staff where it is.
  • Move stock, paper records and electronics off the floor, and keep customers out of wet or slippery areas.
  • Do not run sinks, toilets or dishwashers while a drain is backing up, because every use adds wastewater.
  • Do not touch outlets, panels or equipment near standing water. Turn off the breaker only if you can reach it dry.
  • Photograph the damage and the shutoff valve, and note the time you first saw the problem.

What makes a commercial plumbing job cost more or less

Commercial work varies widely, so the price depends on the building and the problem.

  • Size of the pipe and equipment Larger lines need heavier machines and more time to work through.
  • Access and building hours Working around customers or tenants can slow a job down.
  • Cause of the failure A worn washer and a broken main take very different effort.
  • Parts and fixtures Commercial-grade fixtures can cost more and take longer to source.
  • Time of day Emergency after-hours work is scheduled differently from a booked visit.
