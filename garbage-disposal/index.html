Garbage disposal repair that starts with safety

Disposals fail loudly or not at all. One that hums but will not turn, one that leaks onto the cabinet floor, or one that is dead after a bang from inside all need attention. Call (970) 708-6013 and the 24/7 line will send a plumber, including nights, weekends and holidays.

Before anything else, switch the unit off at the wall and at the breaker, and keep your hands out of the sink opening. Even a disposal that seems dead can start without warning if a switch is flipped.

A leaking disposal is an emergency, here is the order

Water under the sink damages cabinets quickly, so work through these in order.

  • Turn the disposal off at the switch and at the breaker panel.
  • Close the shutoff valves under the sink if the dishwasher or faucet is feeding water into the leak.
  • Place a bucket under the unit and empty the cabinet so nothing soaks up the water.
  • Do not run the dishwasher or the faucet until the plumber has seen the leak.
  • If you smell burning or see smoke, leave the area, switch off the breaker if it is safe, and call 911.

Night and weekend calls reach the same 24/7 line. The plumber finds the source of the leak, which may be the unit itself, a seal, a drain pipe or the dishwasher hose.

Repair or replace the unit

A jammed disposal that frees up with a wrench and spins normally can often be kept. A unit that leaks from the housing cannot be fixed, because the casing is sealed.

Age and the cost of repair also matter. A disposal that has been jammed several times, runs weakly or rattles is usually near the end of its life. The plumber will tell you which side of the line yours is on, and you decide.

If the disposal is only a few years old and the fault is a loose connection or a seal, repair makes sense. Replacement is the better call when the motor is dead or the casing leaks.

What replacing a disposal involves

  1. Power and water off.

    The unit is switched off at the breaker and the supply valves are closed.

  2. Remove the old unit.

    The drain pipe, dishwasher hose and mounting ring are disconnected and the old disposal comes out.

  3. Mount the new one.

    The flange is sealed to the sink and the new unit is attached and connected to the drain.

  4. Reconnect and test.

    The dishwasher hose and electrical connection are restored, then the plumber runs water and the unit to check for leaks.

If a sink needs a disposal for the first time, an electrician or a wiring change may be needed. Tell the dispatcher so the visit is planned properly.

Kitchen sink clogs that look like disposal trouble

Standing water in one or both sink bowls is not always the disposal. A grease clog beyond the unit or a blocked branch line can make the water rise even though the motor runs fine. The plumber checks the disposal first, then follows the line.

Kitchen drains clog more often than most others, so a disposal that seems to fail may simply be sitting on top of a blocked pipe. Drain cleaning explains how those lines are cleared, and the same visit can cover both.

Dishwashers and other connections

A disposal is usually tied to the dishwasher, so a leak at that hose can look like a disposal problem. The dishwasher drain often connects through a small port on the disposal body, and a loose clamp or cracked hose leaks onto the cabinet floor.

If a new sink, counter or dishwasher has been installed, ask the plumber to check the reconnected water line and drain. Poorly connected supply lines and drains cause leaks that show up days later.

What changes the price of disposal work

A simple unjam and a full replacement are different jobs, so the price depends on what is wrong.

  • Repair or replacement A reset or unjam is quicker than removing the old unit and mounting a new one.
  • Size and power of the unit Larger motors and quieter models cost more.
  • Mounting and wiring Older mounts or missing outlets can add steps to the install.
  • Connected parts A bad dishwasher hose or drain pipe may need replacing as well.
  • When you call After-hours emergencies are dispatched differently from daytime visits.

Most homeowners policies cover sudden accidental water damage but not wear-related repair, and a worn disposal is usually treated as wear. Read your own policy to see what yours says. Call (970) 708-6013 for a clear price before work starts.

What to keep out of a disposal

Most disposal failures start with something that never should have gone down.

  • Grease, oil and fat, which cool and coat the pipe past the unit.
  • Coffee grounds, eggshells and starchy foods that pack into a paste.
  • Fibrous items such as celery, corn husks and onion skins.
  • Bones, fruit pits and hard shells that jam the blades.
  • Non-food items such as glass, metal and plastic.

Run cold water before, during and after each use so the waste moves down the line instead of settling.
