When a water heater problem is an emergency

A water heater that has quit is a nuisance. A tank that is leaking or smells like gas is a different call. Emergency repair is available 24/7, nights and weekends included, and this is what to do while you wait for the plumber.

  • If you smell gas or rotten eggs near a gas heater, leave the building first. Call the gas utility or 911 from outside, then call us. Do not look for the source and do not flip switches.
  • For a leaking tank with no gas smell, close the cold water valve on the pipe feeding the top of the heater. The handle turns clockwise until it stops.
  • Turn off the power at the breaker for an electric heater, or set a gas heater's control to off. A dry heating element can burn out in minutes.
  • Keep pets and children away from the pooled water and from any hot discharge pipe.
  • Move boxes and stored items out of the puddle, and open the nearest hose bib or floor drain if you can do it safely and know where the water is headed.

Call (970) 708-6013 once those steps are done. Tell the dispatcher the fuel type, how big the leak is and whether the tank is in a garage, closet, basement or attic.

A puddle under the tank: where the water is really coming from

Water on the floor near a heater is not always the tank. Loose supply connections, a dripping relief valve, a leaking drain valve and condensation on a cold tank can all leave a puddle, and each is a smaller repair than a failed tank.

The plumber dries the base, runs the heater, and watches where the first drop forms. A leak from the top fittings is a quick fix. Water that seeps from the bottom jacket or from under the insulation usually means the steel tank has rusted through.

Some callers find water at the edge of a garage slab or along a foundation wall, away from the heater. That can still be the heater, but it can also be a supply line. If the tank checks out dry, leak detection is the next step.

Gas, electric and hybrid heaters: what gets repaired

Gas heaters fail at the burner side. A pilot that will not stay lit, a worn thermocouple, a dirty burner or a faulty gas control valve cause most no-heat calls. Gas work is done with the fuel shut off, and the plumber checks the flue draws properly before leaving.

Electric heaters fail at the elements and thermostats. Two elements sit inside the tank, and when the lower one fails the tank gives a little hot water and then runs cold. Testing each part with a meter takes minutes and avoids guessing.

Hybrid heat pump units add a compressor and a fan, and error codes are more common. Bring the model name from the label and tell the dispatcher any code on the display. Tankless units are repaired differently, so see tankless water heater service.

Quick checks before the plumber arrives

These take two minutes and give the plumber a head start. Do not open any panel on a gas unit, and stop if anything smells wrong.

  • Look at the label for the fuel type, tank size and manufacture date, and read them to the dispatcher.
  • Check the breaker for an electric unit. A tripped breaker that trips again after a reset means a short, so leave it off.
  • Note whether hot water is missing at every tap or only one. One tap points to a fixture, not the heater.
  • Look at the discharge pipe beside the tank for a steady drip or a trickle.
  • Write down any code on the display of a hybrid or electronic-ignition unit.

Photos of the label and the puddle are useful, but do not climb on anything or reach behind a hot tank to take them.

What changes the cost of a water heater repair

A repair can be a ten-minute part swap or the start of a replacement decision. These are the factors that move the bill.

  • The failed part A thermocouple, element or relief valve is a small repair. A gas control valve or a leaking tank is not.
  • Age of the tank On an old tank, sinking money into repairs may not make sense when the steel is near the end of its life.
  • Fuel type and model Gas, electric and hybrid units use different parts, and some parts take longer to source.
  • Access A heater in a tight closet or an attic takes more time to work on than one in an open garage.
  • Time of the call A cold-morning emergency call is handled on a different schedule than a daytime booking.

Insurance tends to respond to a tank that suddenly bursts, not to a part that wore out. Your own policy has the final word on that. Call (970) 708-6013 for a real number, and the price is clear before work starts.

After the repair: what to expect from the heater

A good visit ends with hot water at the tap, a check of the relief valve and a look at the venting or wiring. The plumber should tell you straight if the tank has years left or is on borrowed time.

Flushing a few gallons from the drain valve once a year carries sediment out before it hardens. If your tank sits in a finished area, a drain pan with a line to a floor drain limits the damage when it eventually fails.
