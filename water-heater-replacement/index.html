A tank that has split open or is spilling water onto the floor cannot be saved, and every hour it runs makes the damage worse. If the symptom is only that the water runs lukewarm or the pilot keeps dying, water heater repair may be all you need.

If your tank is flooding, treat it as an emergency

A failing tank can release dozens of gallons quickly. Work through this list in order while you wait.

  • Gas smell or a hissing sound from the burner area means leave the building first. Call the gas utility or 911 from outside and do not search for the cause.
  • Close the cold water supply valve on the pipe entering the top of the tank. If it will not turn, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  • Switch off the breaker for an electric heater, and keep your hands out of any water on the floor near it.
  • Lift or slide belongings out of the water's path and clear the floor drain of anything that blocks it.
  • Take photos of the tank and the damage for your own records before anything is moved.

Night and weekend calls reach the same line. The plumber can usually plan a same-day swap when the supplier and the tank size cooperate, and tells you the timing on the phone.

How a replacement visit goes

The work follows the same order each time, so there are few surprises.

  1. Shut down and drain.

    Water, power or gas are shut off, and the tank is drained to a floor drain or outside.

  2. Remove the old unit.

    Connections are cut loose and the old tank is carried out, along with anything damaged beneath it.

  3. Set and connect.

    The new tank goes on a level base, with new supply connectors, a relief valve and discharge pipe.

  4. Check venting and fuel.

    A gas unit gets a leak test on the gas line and a check that exhaust leaves the house properly.

  5. Fill, fire up and test.

    The tank fills before power is applied, then the plumber confirms temperature and walks you through the shutoffs.

Choosing the new unit: tank, tankless or heat pump

Most emergency swaps put a like-for-like tank back, because it fits the existing venting, gas line and space. That keeps the job short and the hot water coming back fast.

When there is time to plan, other options open up. Tankless units heat water on demand and free floor space, but need the right gas line size and venting. A heat pump water heater uses electricity far more efficiently, and it also needs room and airflow around it. If you want to move from gas to a heat pump, tell the dispatcher before the visit.

Size matters more than most callers expect. A tank too small for a family runs out mid-shower, and one too large costs more to heat all day. The plumber sizes it by household and peak use, not by what the old label says.

What drives the price of a replacement

Quotes vary because the jobs vary. These are the pieces that move the total.

  • Tank size and type A larger tank, a heat pump or a tankless unit all cost more than a basic tank of the same capacity.
  • Fuel and venting Switching fuel or rerouting a flue adds parts and labor.
  • What is behind the old unit Rotted shutoffs, old connectors or a damaged pan are often found once the tank is out.
  • Access Attic installs and tight closets take longer than a garage floor.
  • Permits and code updates Ask the Town of Johnstown building department whether your job needs a permit.
  • Time of the call After-hours dispatch carries different pricing than a swap booked days ahead.
