First steps in a plumbing emergency

What you do in the first five minutes decides how much damage you end up with. Match your situation below, then call the 24/7 line.

  • Water is running and will not stop. Shut off the main water valve where the line enters the house. If you cannot find it, turn off the valve at the water heater and call.
  • Sewage or wastewater is coming up through a drain. Stop using every sink, tub, toilet and the washing machine. Keep children and pets out of the wet area.
  • There is water near outlets, the panel or an appliance. Turn off power to that area at the breaker only if you can reach it without standing in water. Otherwise stay back.
  • You smell gas. Leave the building right away and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not look for the source and do not call a plumber first.
  • A pipe froze and nothing is flowing. Leave the faucet open, keep the heat on, and call before you try to thaw anything with a flame or torch.

What Johnstown homes ask of a plumber

Johnstown was platted in 1902 along Parish Avenue and sits just west of the point where the Big Thompson and Little Thompson rivers meet. The oldest houses around Downtown Johnstown were built long before modern plumbing codes, so galvanized supply pipe and aging sewer lines are worth watching in the older blocks.

Most of the town is far newer. Johnstown counted 9,887 residents in 2010, 17,303 in the 2020 census and an estimated 22,433 more recently, and subdivisions such as Thompson River Ranch, Pioneer Ridge, Carlson Farms and Rolling Hills Ranch fill the growth. Those homes bring a different set of calls, mostly water heaters, fixtures and the first signs of builder-grade parts wearing out.

The town's position along Interstate 25 and U.S. Route 34 also matters when a plumber is coming. Johnson's Corner, the truck stop near the I-25 and US-34 interchange, is a familiar landmark for drivers, and it makes directions easy when you are describing where the house is.

How to check any plumber's license

Plumbers in Colorado are licensed through the state. You can confirm any plumber, ours or anyone else's, on the Colorado DORA license lookup, and the Colorado State Plumbing Board explains the license types.

Permit rules vary by job and by town, so ask the Town of Johnstown building department whether your project needs one. A plumber who is licensed and insured will know the answer for most common repairs.
