Emergency sewer line cleaning in Johnstown, CO

A blocked sewer line is one of the few plumbing problems that cannot wait. If sewage is backing up into a tub, a shower or a basement floor drain, call (970) 708-6013 now. Emergency sewer line cleaning is available 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays.

Sewer line cleaning means clearing the large pipe that carries everything from the house out to the municipal main. When it clogs, the water has only one place to go, which is back up through the lowest drain in the building.

Sewer backup: what to do first

While you wait for the plumber, these steps limit the mess and keep your household safe.

  • Stop all water use. That includes toilets, showers, the dishwasher and the washing machine.
  • Keep children, pets and anyone with a weak immune system away from sewage. Wash hands carefully after any contact.
  • Move boxes, rugs and stored items from the floor of a basement or garage, and keep clear of outlets and appliances near the water.
  • Do not pour bleach or drain chemicals into the backup. They will not clear a main line and they create fumes.
  • Locate your cleanout if you can and leave the cap in place. Tell the dispatcher where it is.
  • Take photos of the damage for your insurance claim before cleanup starts.

Night calls and holiday calls go to the same line. The plumber clears the blockage first, then explains what caused it and whether the line should be inspected.

How the line gets cleared

  1. Reach the line.

    The plumber opens the cleanout or removes a toilet so the equipment can enter the main.

  2. Cut through the blockage.

    A powered cable with the right cutter breaks up roots, wipes and packed debris until the line moves.

  3. Scour if needed.

    Where grease or scale coats the pipe, a jetter washes the walls so the line stays open longer.

  4. Confirm the flow.

    Water runs through every fixture to make sure the line drains freely before the plumber leaves.

When the same blockage keeps coming back, ask about a camera. A short camera inspection shows whether roots, a bellied pipe or a broken joint is behind it.

Cleaning the main line versus repairing it

Clearing a blockage and repairing a damaged pipe are different jobs. Cleaning removes what is in the line. Repair fixes the pipe itself when it is cracked, offset or collapsed.

Many main line stoppages are only a cleaning job, especially in newer lines with wipes or grease behind them. A line that clogs again and again may be telling you the pipe is damaged, and that is the point to talk about sewer line repair instead of paying for another cleaning.

Sometimes the plumber will not know until the line is open and moving. Honest advice comes after the cable has passed through and the camera has had a look.

Roots, wipes and grease, the three usual causes

Roots enter through tiny gaps in joints, then grow into thick mats that catch everything passing through. Cutting them restores flow, but unless the joint is repaired they return, sometimes within a year or two.

Wipes marketed as flushable do not break down like paper, and they tangle around each other until they form a rope. Grease is the quiet one, building thin layers on the pipe wall that narrow the opening until a normal evening of laundry fills the line.

Knowing which of the three you have shapes the fix. A rope of wipes pulls out. Roots call for a cutter and a long-term plan. Grease responds well to hydro jetting.

What changes the price of sewer line cleaning

Every main line is different, so the final number depends on conditions in the pipe.

  • Access to the line A cleanout at the right spot saves time. Without one, the plumber may have to pull a toilet.
  • How far the blockage sits A blockage near the house is quicker to reach than one near the street.
  • What is blocking it Wipes, roots and packed grease need different tools and different amounts of time.
  • Whether a camera is added Looking inside the pipe after clearing it adds a step and shows what to do next.
  • Hour of the call An after-hours emergency is scheduled differently from a daytime visit.

Most homeowners policies treat sewer backup as a separate endorsement and do not cover it by default, and they rarely cover the cost of the cleaning itself. Read your own policy and ask your insurer what applies. Call (970) 708-6013 for a clear price before work starts.

Habits that keep a sewer line open

  • Flush toilet paper only, and bin everything else, including wipes labeled flushable.
  • Scrape plates and pans into the trash so grease and food do not reach the drain.
  • Know where your cleanout is and keep it clear of boxes and landscaping.
  • Watch for gurgling or slow drains in more than one place, and call before a backup starts.
  • Call 811 at least two full business days before digging near the line. It is free.
