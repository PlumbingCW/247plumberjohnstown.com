From Hillsboro to today: what Milliken's age means for your pipes

Milliken began as Hillsboro, an agricultural trading post that grew up around a railroad in the 1860s. The town was named for Judge John D. Milliken and incorporated on October 1, 1910. Fires in the 1910s destroyed much of the early town, and the railroad was abandoned in 1917.

The oldest homes near the historic center date from a different era than the subdivisions that came later. Pipe of that age can fail slowly and then all at once. If your Milliken home is several decades old and the water has started to look rusty or the pressure has dropped, repiping is worth pricing before a pipe bursts.

If a pipe bursts or a leak will not stop in Milliken

Do these things first, in this order, while you wait for the plumber.

  • Shut off the main water valve. It is usually where the supply line enters the house, in a utility room, basement or crawl space.
  • Kill power to any wet area. If water is near outlets or the panel, leave the breaker off and stay out of standing water.
  • Clear the immediate area. Lift rugs and electronics, and move people and pets away from the wet floor.
  • Open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to drain the lines, then call the 24/7 line and give your Milliken street address.

Tell the dispatcher what you can see and where the water is coming from. A photo of the leak and the shutoff valve helps the plumber plan before arriving.

Groundwater, basements and the 2013 floods

Flooding in September 2013 affected 3,000 homes in Weld County, and Weld County ground took on a great deal of water. The practical lesson for a homeowner is that ground in this part of the county can saturate quickly after heavy rain.

If your Milliken home has a basement, test the sump pump before the wet season by pouring water into the pit and watching it start and empty. A pump that does not run is a better problem to find on a dry day than during a storm.
