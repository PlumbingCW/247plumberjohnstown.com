A growing town and the plumbing calls that come with it

Mead counted 4,781 people in the 2020 census and an estimated 6,450 in 2023. That is a jump of roughly a third in three years, and a town that adds homes that fast also adds water heaters, sump pits, outdoor taps and irrigation lines that all need service at some point.

Newer homes are not trouble-free. A fitting that was set badly, a pump that never got tested or a heater that was oversized can fail early. If you bought recently and something is already leaking, call the 24/7 line at (970) 708-6013 and describe what you see.

Mead through the year, season by season

Colorado weather decides a lot of what breaks. Here is what tends to go wrong in each season and what to do about it.

  1. Spring melt and storms.

    Heavy rain and snowmelt send water toward foundations. Pour a bucket into the sump pit to confirm the pump starts, and if it does not, read about sump pump repair.

  2. Summer demand.

    Irrigation runs hard and water pressure swings. Walk the yard, look for soggy ground near the meter and treat a spinning meter with no one home as a leak.

  3. Fall prep.

    Disconnect hoses, close the indoor valve to outdoor taps and drain irrigation lines. Take a look at the water heater for rust at the base.

  4. Winter freeze.

    Open cabinet doors on exterior walls during hard cold, and keep a slow drip on at risk taps. A frozen line that splits shows up as water the minute it thaws.

If water is coming through a ceiling, wall or floor in Mead

You are looking at a few minutes of useful action. Spend them like this.

  • Close the main water valve. In many homes it sits near the water heater, at the front wall or in the garage.
  • Flip off power to the room if water is dripping near lights or outlets, and do not stand in a pool to do it.
  • Catch what you can in buckets and move furniture, bedding and electronics clear of the drip.
  • Open the lowest faucet to relieve the pressure left in the lines.
  • Call the 24/7 line, give your Mead street address and say where the water started.

For a water heater that is leaking and also smells of gas, leave the building before anything else, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

Which water district feeds your Mead address

Mead gets water from the Little Thompson Water District and the Longs Peak Water District. Which one serves a given house depends on where it sits, so look at your water bill for the name of the provider.

That bill matters when a leak is outside your house. Water leaking on the utility side of the meter belongs to the district, so call them first, and the dispatcher can tell you which side of the line you are on. Anything past the meter is yours to fix.
