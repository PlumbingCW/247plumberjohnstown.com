Before We Arrive: first five minutes of a Berthoud leak

Water moves fast, so take these steps in order while the plumber drives over.

  • Turn the main water valve clockwise until it stops. Look where the line comes through the foundation wall or floor, often near the water heater.
  • Switch off breakers that serve the wet room. If the panel itself is damp, do not touch it and tell the dispatcher.
  • Pull valuables, rugs and boxes away from the spreading water, and keep kids and pets out of the room.
  • Run the lowest tap in the house until it stops flowing so the pipes drain down.
  • Call the line, give your Berthoud address and describe what you see. A quick phone photo of the break is useful.

If you smell gas at any point, leave the house first and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

A downtown that was carried up the bluff, and what that means for you

Berthoud grew around the Colorado Central Railroad in 1877, and in 1883 and 1884 its buildings were moved to the bluff north of the river. The town was incorporated on August 28, 1888. Streets that old have seen decades of repairs, so a house near the center can hold a mix of pipe from several eras.

The practical step is to look before you guess. Find the pipe where water enters your house and note whether it is copper, gray threaded steel or flexible plastic. If you find steel and the water has gone brown, ask for repiping advice before the next failure picks the time for you.

Outdoor taps, irrigation and the Garden Spot habit

Berthoud was long called the Garden Spot of Colorado, with sugar beets among the crops that built the town. Plenty of yards here still carry hose bibs, sprinkler zones and garden taps. Each one is a spot where water sits in a pipe that can freeze.

Before the first hard freeze, disconnect hoses and shut off any indoor valve feeding an outside spigot. A cracked hose bib often shows itself only when you turn it on in spring, so a wet wall or ceiling near an outdoor tap is worth a call to leak detection.
